# Authored surface orientation

The v1 importer records `ThemeSource.surfaceScheme` as the original palette's detected light or dark scheme. Registry sources preserve their existing gradient positions, color references, geometry, interpolation, alpha, lens and variation values; the orientation field does not rewrite the original paint.

Surface orientation is independent of role-map mirroring. A dark-authored theme such as Velvet uses a complete explicit dark role map and `mirrorDark: false`, which previously also disabled surface inversion in its light presentation. The resolver now reflects the authored surface tone positions only when the requested scheme differs from `surfaceScheme`. Semantic role references follow the selected role map and are not reflected twice. Missing surfaces retain the default semantic surface behavior.

For explicitly oriented literal surface stops, the resolver measures the literal's position on the source ramp, reflects that position, retains the literal hue and its fraction of available chroma, and maps the resulting color into gamut. The literal is resolved unchanged in the authored scheme. This allows Whiteout's authored near-white page literal to have a dark tonal counterpart without altering its original light paint. Raw artwork layers remain unchanged. A source lacking the optional orientation field retains the older resolution behavior and literal warning, so the change does not infer undocumented art direction for imported arbitrary v2 sources.

Backdrop scene palettes and mark artwork are outside this surface correction. `resolveBackdrop` resolves explicit tones and literals as authored; semantic role palette entries follow the selected roles. Whiteout's explicit base/highlight palette remains unchanged in both schemes. Its default semantic accent/veil palette entries can follow the selected roles.
